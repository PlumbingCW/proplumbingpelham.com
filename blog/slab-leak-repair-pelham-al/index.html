Slab Leak Repair in Pelham, AL: Signs, Detection, Options

How to tell a leak under a concrete floor from other problems, how plumbers pinpoint it without breaking the whole slab, and the repair choices that follow.

Call (855) 575-2890

If you suspect a leak, close the main valve and see whether the meter dial keeps turning.

Warm floor, damp carpet edge, hissing in a quiet house, or a meter that moves with everything off.

Photos, invoices, and bills from before and after matter to your insurer and to the water utility.

By the Pelham Pro Plumbing team. Published October 15, 2026. Updated October 15, 2026.

A slab leak is a leak in a water line that runs under or inside a concrete slab foundation. Because the pipe is buried in or beneath the concrete, you rarely see the water until it has been running a while. Many homeowners notice the bill first. This guide covers how the leak shows itself, how a plumber finds the exact spot, and what the repair choices really involve.

It applies only if your home is built on a concrete slab, or has a slab section such as an addition or a garage. If you are not sure what is under your floor, a plumber can tell you in a short visit, and the answer matters before you plan a repair.

What a leak under the floor tends to look like

The symptoms overlap with other problems, so look for a pattern rather than a single clue. Warm spots on a floor that are not near a heater suggest a leak on the hot line. Damp or discolored carpet along a wall, a musty smell, or lifting flooring edges are other signs.

  • A faint hissing or running sound when every tap is off
  • A water meter dial that keeps moving with all fixtures closed
  • Hot water that arrives lukewarm or takes much longer than usual
  • Low pressure at several taps that is new
  • A jump in the water bill with no change in how you use water

Any one of these has other explanations, such as a running toilet or a failing water heater. Several together make a slab leak much more likely.

Rule out the easy suspects, then confirm

Start with the meter. Turn off every fixture and appliance, find your water meter, and watch the small low-flow dial or leak indicator for a few minutes. If it spins, water is escaping somewhere past the meter. Next, close the main shutoff valve at the house and check again.

Before you trust the dial, check for outdoor sprinklers, an ice maker or a softener that may be using water, and rule them out first. If you cannot find the main shutoff valve, call the 24/7 line and the dispatcher will talk you through finding it.

If the dial stops after you close the main, the leak is inside your home's plumbing. If it keeps moving with the main closed, the leak may be on the stretch between the meter and your house. A plumber can help you tell which side of the house the leak is on. Confirm the boundary with Pelham Water Works at (205) 620-6420 and ask who is responsible for that stretch of pipe before you pay for work.

How a plumber finds the spot without demolition

Breaking up concrete on a hunch is a poor way to find a leak. Modern pinpointing uses tools that sense what the pipe is doing. Acoustic listening equipment picks up the sound of water escaping through a small hole, thermal cameras show warm patches on hot line breaks, and pressure testing isolates which section of the system is losing water.

For a hot line, a thermal scan can map the route the pipe takes under the floor. Combined with listening, these tools can often narrow the leak to a small area. Ask the plumber to confirm the location before anyone cuts the slab, so the opening stays as small as the job needs. See leak detection for how that works.

Common ways to fix a leak under the slab

Which repair fits depends on how many leaks there are, how old and what type the pipe is, and what the floor above is made of. A plumber should lay out the options and say why one fits your house. Pipe lining is another option some trades offer, so ask whether it applies.

Paperwork that helps you later

Photograph the damp area, the meter reading, and the repair opening before anything is closed up. Keep the plumber's written report and invoice. Ask your insurer what your policy covers before work starts, and keep photos and the plumber's invoice.

Do not wait for approval if water is actively escaping. Shutting off the main valve is the usual first step. If it will not turn, do not force it, and call a plumber. If water is near outlets or the panel, stay clear and switch off power only if you can reach the panel from dry footing. For an active leak or a bill that keeps climbing, call the 24/7 line and a plumber can start with detection.

Pelham water bills, leak adjustments, and ground that moves

Pelham Water Works has a leak adjustment policy, and a leak under a slab is the kind of hidden leak it was written for, though the city decides eligibility. According to the city's policy page, it looks at whether your usage ran above 1.5 times the average of the preceding three billing cycles, and that the leak was not readily detectable. This is policy at time of writing, so confirm current terms with Pelham Water Works at (205) 620-6420.
