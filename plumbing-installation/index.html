Plumbing installation in Pelham, AL means putting in new water supply lines, drains, vents, and fixtures, either in a remodel or a new space. It is planned work, scheduled around your build or renovation. Call us with a short list of what you are adding, and a licensed and insured plumber will talk through the sequence, the materials, and who should be on site for each stage.

Rough-in, then finish: how the work is sequenced

Larger installations usually have two stages. A single fixture swap is one visit. Rough-in comes first, when the framing is open. The plumber sets drain pipe on a steady downhill slope, runs supply lines, installs vents, and caps the stub-outs where fixtures will go. Nothing is hidden yet, so it is the easiest time to change your mind.

Finish comes after drywall, tile, and paint. This is when toilets, sinks, faucets, and tubs are set, connected, and tested. The two visits can be weeks apart. A drawing that shows the exact fixture locations and heights helps both. Tell the plumber about tile thickness, vanity depth, and the faucet model before the rough-in is set.

Alabama's Plumbers and Gas Fitters Examining Board rule 720-X-1-.02 names the 2021 International Residential Code (IRC) for residential plumbing, and the board can tell you the current edition.

Kitchens and bathrooms in a remodel

A kitchen needs a faucet supply, a drain, a dishwasher connection, and a disposal if you want one. A bath needs a vanity, a toilet, and a tub or shower valve. Decide on the fixtures first, since each has its own rough-in dimensions.

Shower valves are the part to settle early. They sit behind tile, so a valve in the wrong place or at the wrong depth means opening the wall again. If you are choosing a toilet, measure the rough-in distance and see toilet repair and installation for what a toilet set involves. A faucet upgrade is covered under faucet repair.

Moving a fixture or adding a bathroom

Moving a toilet or a sink changes the drain, and drains depend on slope. A fixture far from the vent or the main stack may need a new vent run, and that can mean opening a floor or a ceiling. A fixture on the other side of a wall is usually easier than one in a different part of the house.

Adding a bathroom is a larger job. It needs a drain to the main, a vent, hot and cold supply, and often a floor opening in a slab or crawlspace. Check that the water heater can handle the added demand, and if it cannot, water heater installation belongs in the plan. The City's Building Department page mentions a sewer impact fee when a project connects to sewer. Ask the department at 205.620.6495 whether it applies to your project.

Laundry hookups and outdoor spigots

A washer needs two supply valves and a standpipe drain with a trap. The box on the wall that holds them should be set at the height of the machine and be reachable without moving the washer. A drain pan under a washer on an upper floor is a sensible extra.

An outdoor spigot, or hose bib, runs a supply line through the exterior wall to a valve outside. Frost-proof designs put the shutoff inside the warm wall. Whichever type you choose, disconnect the hose when you are not using it, since a hose left on can trap water against the valve.

Tying new lines into old pipe

New supply lines in a remodel usually connect to whatever pipe is already in the wall. Ask the plumber what that existing pipe is made of before you choose a route, because the join and the fittings depend on it.

If the old pipe looks tired, the better plan may be to replace the whole run instead of tying new pipe into it. See repiping for how the pipe materials compare for whole-house work.

Working with your general contractor

If a contractor runs the job, the plumber works to their schedule and framing. Share the fixture list and the water heater location early. Ask who is responsible for patching after the rough-in, who orders the fixtures, and who arranges inspection.

If you are running the job yourself, plan the order of work with the plumber. Rough-in happens before insulation, and finish happens after paint. Keep the receipts and model numbers for the fixtures you buy.

Permits and the Pelham Building Department

The department at 3162 Pelham Pkwy issues building permits and inspects commercial and residential construction inside city limits. Contractors apply through the Citizen Access Portal, and permit fees are based on the square footage of the improvement, so describe the size of your project when you ask. The office is open Monday to Friday, 7:30 to 4:30, and inspections run Monday to Friday, mornings and afternoons.
