What happens when you call

Our dispatcher answers and asks four plain things. What is wrong, where is the water or the backup, what have you already shut off, and what is the address. Short answers are fine. You do not need the right plumbing words.

Next, the dispatcher tells you what to do while you wait. For a leak that usually means the main valve, and for a stopped drain it means no more water down any fixture. If electrical outlets are near standing water, keep people out of the room.

Same-day appointments are offered when available, and for urgent calls dispatch works to send someone as soon as the schedule allows. We do not promise an arrival time, because distance and open calls change it. The dispatcher will tell you where things stand.

Once the plumber has seen the problem, you hear a clear price before work starts. If the plan changes after a wall is opened or a camera finds something new, ask the plumber to walk you through the new price before work continues. There are no forms and no booking portal, just the phone.

What we fix

Most calls fall into a few groups. Water that should not be there comes first, with burst pipe repair for a line that has split and leak detection for the one nobody can find. A rising bill with no wet spot is covered by meter leak testing, and a leak between the street and the house is a water service line job.

Drains are the second group. A clog in one sink is a job for drain cleaning, and a line that keeps clogging may need hydro jetting to scour the pipe wall. When the trouble is the line leaving the house, sewer line repair covers cracked or collapsed sections.

Hot water is the third. A tank with no heat or a drip at the valve can often go to water heater repair, while an old tank that has failed means water heater replacement. Some households choose a tankless water heater instead, which is planned work rather than an emergency.

Fixtures round out the list. We handle toilet repair, faucet and sink repair and garbage disposal repair. Businesses with a restroom, a mop sink or a kitchen line can ask about commercial plumbing.

There are limits worth stating. We do not install or pump septic tanks, and electrical faults need an electrician. If you are not sure which category you are in, call and describe it, because the dispatcher can sort that out in a minute.

What shapes the price of a plumbing job

A firm price needs a plumber to see the job. Dispatch can explain by phone what moves the price, but does not quote one. These are the factors.

AccessA pipe under an open crawlspace is quicker to reach than one behind a finished wall or under a slab.
Cause and extentA single clogged trap costs less labor than a collapsed section of line, even if both look like a slow drain from above.
Parts and materialsThe size, type and availability of pipe, valves and fittings change the total, as does a fixture's age.
Condition of what is thereCorroded valves and seized fittings take longer to work on than clean ones, and nobody knows until the part is exposed.
Scope of repairRepairing a section, replacing a run, or opening and closing walls each add different amounts of labor.

Ask your insurer what your policy covers and keep photos and the plumber's invoice. For a real number on your job, call the 24/7 line and describe what you see.

How to check any plumber's license

As general advice for any Alabama homeowner, look up the plumber before work begins. The Alabama Plumbers and Gas Fitters Examining Board runs a licensee search at pgfb.alabama.gov, and it covers individuals and businesses. Ask the person at your door for a name and look them up while they wait.

That check takes two minutes and applies to every plumber, including us. Also ask the plumber to walk you through the price before work starts.
