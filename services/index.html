Emergency or planned: how to decide

Some problems cannot wait for morning. Water pouring through a ceiling, sewage rising in a floor drain, a split pipe that will not stop flowing, or a tank leaking across the garage floor are emergencies. Shut off the main water valve if the problem is water, stop using every drain if it is sewage, and call the 24/7 line. Emergency dispatch runs nights and weekends too.

Planned work is anything stable. A dripping faucet, a toilet that runs after every flush, a disposal that hums, or an old water heater you want to swap before it fails can all wait for a same-day appointment when available, or for the next opening. Planned work gives you time to compare options and pick a time that suits you. It is also easier to price, because the plumber is not working around standing water.

The gray zone is a problem that is slow but getting worse. A drain that now gurgles, a ceiling stain that grows after every shower, or a water bill that jumped with no change in habits can look small and turn into damage. If you are unsure, call and describe what you see. Describe what you see and the dispatcher will tell you whether to treat it as an emergency.

How the nineteen services group together

Drains and sewer work covers everything that carries water out of the house. Drain cleaning handles a clogged sink, tub or toilet drain, and hydro jetting uses pressurized water to scour pipe walls when a snake only punches a hole through the clog. Farther down the line sit sewer line cleaning, sewer camera inspection and sewer line repair.

Pelham Water Works runs sewer as well as water, so a backup in several fixtures may be on your side of the property line or the city's. Describe the backup on the call and the plumber can help work out which it is before anyone starts digging.

Water heater work has four pages. Water heater repair fits a unit with a fixable fault, and replacement fits a tank that has failed. Planned swaps go through water heater installation, and tankless water heaters have their own page because venting, gas supply and sizing differ from a tank.

Leaks and supply lines include burst pipe repair, leak detection, water service line repair and replacement, and repiping. For a high water bill, water meter leak detection covers the paperwork Pelham Water Works asks for, which is policy at time of writing, so confirm current terms with the city. Fixtures cover toilets, faucets and sinks, and garbage disposals. Installs and commercial work covers plumbing installation, such as new fixtures and rough-in, and commercial plumbing for shops and offices.

Work that lives inside other pages

Some services have no page of their own because they are sections of a larger one. Cast iron pipe repair is covered on sewer line repair, and hybrid, electric and gas water heater repair is covered on water heater repair. For specialist items on a commercial plumbing job, such as backflow devices, grease traps or code checks, the plumber will tell you what applies. Ask Pelham Water Works or the Pelham Building Department for local requirements.

What we do not take on

We do not install or pump septic tanks, and electrical work needs an electrician. If a plumbing problem turns out to involve wiring, such as a water heater with a dead heating element circuit, the plumber will say so and tell you what to hand off. Permit rules vary by job, so ask the Pelham Building Department at 205.620.6495 before you plan a larger project.
