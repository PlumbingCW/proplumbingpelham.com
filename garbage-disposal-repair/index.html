Garbage disposal repair means fixing the grinder, seals, and connections under the sink, or replacing the unit. Stop running water, switch the disposal off at the wall or breaker, and call us from a dry spot.

Emergency leaks and backups under the sink

Two situations cannot wait. One is water pouring from the unit onto the cabinet floor, which can swell the base and reach wiring. The other is a sink that fills and will not drain, with the disposal full of dirty water. Both mean the same first move: stop using the sink.

If water is rising rather than leaking, the clog is probably below the disposal in the trap or the branch line, not in the grinder. Running the unit again will not help and may burn out the motor. A plumber can open the trap, clear the line, and tell you whether drain cleaning with a cable is needed beyond it.

A hum, a jam, or no sound at all

A hum with no spin means the grinding plate is stuck. Turn the power off at the switch and at the breaker first. Many units have a hex socket in the center of the underside, and a hex key turned back and forth frees a stuck plate. A light tap on the bottom is not a fix. Check for a lodged spoon or bottle cap with tongs, never fingers.

No sound at all can be a tripped overload. Many disposals have a red reset button on the bottom, but check your unit's manual. With the power off at the switch and the breaker, press it after the motor cools, then restore power and try once. If it trips again straight away, the motor is failing or the jam is worse than it feels. Stop there.

Anything past the plug, such as a dead switch or bad wiring, is electrical work and needs an electrician. A plumber can tell you whether the fault is in the disposal or on the power side. Wiring faults need an electrician.

Stop and call if

  • You smell burning
  • Water is pooling around the base or in the cabinet
  • The unit trips again after one reset
  • You see sparks or the cord or switch is hot or damaged

Finding the leak: flange, body, or dishwasher hose

Dry the whole unit with a rag, then run water into the sink and watch from below. A leak at the very top, where the sink meets the disposal, is the flange, and it needs the plumber's putty or gasket redone. A leak from the side or bottom of the housing often means a cracked body or failed seal, and that unit is usually past repair.

A drip from the short hose on the side is the dishwasher connection, and it only shows when the dishwasher drains. Tightening the clamp or replacing the hose is simple. A leak where the disposal meets the drain pipe is a gasket or a loose mount. Describe the location to the dispatcher so the plumber knows what to look for.

What stays out of the disposal

Fibrous food such as celery, corn husks, and artichokes wraps the plate. Rice, pasta, and coffee grounds swell and pack into a paste in the trap. Bones and fruit pits can jam the motor, and grease cools and coats the pipe walls further down.

Run cold water before, during, and for several seconds after grinding, so scraps move through the trap and away. Wipe greasy pans into the trash first. A weekly flush of ice cubes and a citrus peel freshens the chamber, but it does not clear a slow line.

Swapping in a new disposal

A replacement means a new mount, a drain connection, a dishwasher inlet, and power. Check whether the cabinet has an outlet or a switch before you buy, because hardwired power is electrician work. Match the horsepower to how you cook, and know that a bigger unit takes more space under the sink.

The plumber removes the old unit, cleans the sink opening, sets the new flange, hangs the housing, reconnects the drain, and runs water to test for leaks. Alabama's Plumbers and Gas Fitters Examining Board rule 720-X-1-.02 names the 2021 International Residential Code for residential plumbing, so check the board for the current edition. If the sink is also being replaced, plumbing installation covers the drain and supply work.
