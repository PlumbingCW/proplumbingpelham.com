Sewage coming up through a floor drain or tub is an emergency, and 24/7 emergency dispatch can send a licensed plumber to assess it. Sewer line repair in Pelham, AL means fixing a cracked, rooted, offset, collapsed or sagging pipe between your house and the public sewer, whether by digging, relining or replacing the damaged section. Stop all water use, keep everyone out of the affected rooms, and call and say where the sewage is showing.

Sewer emergency: the first hour with sewage in the house

A backup from a broken line looks like a backup from a plain clog, and you cannot tell them apart from the floor. What you can do is limit the damage. Water you run, including every flush, goes into a pipe that cannot take it.

While you wait for the plumber

Shut off water use and leave the toilets alone. If water stands near outlets, appliances or a panel, keep out of that area and call the 24/7 line. If you can reach the breaker from dry footing, switch off power to the area. Keep children and pets out, and wear boots and gloves for any cleanup.

Night and weekend calls are routine for dispatch, and same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows for repairs that can wait. Note when the backup began and which drain showed it first. The pattern helps the plumber decide between a cleaning and a repair. A cleaning is covered under sewer line cleaning.

What actually breaks a sewer line

Roots are the best known cause. They enter at a joint looking for moisture, grow into a mat, and eventually crack the joint apart. Offset joints happen when soil shifts and two pipe sections no longer line up, leaving a lip that catches paper and wipes.

Bellies, or low sags, happen when the soil beneath the pipe settles. Water pools in the dip, solids settle out, and the line backs up even though nothing is broken at the surface. Heavy loads above a shallow line can crush it, and old cast iron rusts from the inside until the wall flakes or splits.

Camera first, then a decision

No repair should be priced from a guess. A camera on a flexible cable can travel the line and show the damage, and a locator at the surface can mark its depth and position. That tells the plumber whether the fault is a single bad joint or a long deteriorated run, and where a dig would need to start.

A line that is plugged may need clearing before the camera can pass. Footage after cleaning often shows the true state of the pipe. You can read about what the footage shows on the sewer camera inspection page, and it is the same evidence the repair choice rests on.

Dig, trenchless or lining: how the options compare

These are the common repair methods. Which of them a plumber can offer depends on the job, so ask what the footage supports before choosing.

Sewer repair methods and their trade-offs
MethodBest whenTrade-off
Spot dig and replaceOne short damaged section, easy to reachOpens the yard or slab at that point and needs restoring afterward
Trenchless pipe burstingA long failing run with room at both endsNeeds entry pits and a clear path, and it replaces rather than repairs
Cured-in-place liningCracks and root entry in a pipe that still holds its shapeCannot fix a collapse, and the liner narrows the pipe slightly
Full replacement by trenchCollapsed, crushed or badly bellied lineMost disruptive, but gives a new pipe at the right slope

The right choice comes from the footage, not a preference. A pipe that has collapsed cannot be lined, and a pipe with one bad joint rarely needs a long trench. Ask the plumber to point to the problem on the video.

What a repair day involves

Before any digging, 811 is called so buried utilities are marked, and you can ask the plumber who is making that call. Ask what the job includes, which part of the yard, driveway or floor will be opened, and what permits apply. Permit rules vary by job, and the Pelham Building Department at 205.620.6495 can say.

Sewer repair is plumbing work covered by code. The Alabama Plumbers and Gas Fitters Examining Board's rule 720-X-1-.02 names the 2021 International Residential Code (IRC) for residential plumbing and the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) for commercial plumbing, so check the board for the current edition.

How a pipe lining job goes

Lining builds a new pipe inside the old one. The sequence below is the general method, and details vary by the product and the pipe.

  1. Clear and inspect

    The line is cleaned so the liner can bond, and the camera confirms the pipe still holds its round shape.

  2. Prepare the liner

    A flexible tube is soaked in resin and sized to the pipe length.

  3. Pull and inflate

    The tube is pulled into place and inflated so it presses against the pipe wall.

  4. Cure

    Heat, light or time hardens the resin into a smooth new pipe inside the old one.

  5. Reopen and check

    Side connections are cut open and a final camera run confirms the result.

Cast iron in crawlspaces and under slabs

Cast iron drain pipe lasts a long time, but it rusts from the inside, and scale builds up until the bore narrows. Flaking scale and a roughened bore are what the camera shows. Where cast iron runs under a slab, the plumber weighs breaking the floor against relining the old pipe in place.

Where it is reachable in a crawlspace, a failed section can be cut out and replaced with new pipe and couplings. A camera shows how far the damage runs, and that decides between a small repair and replacing the whole stack. Heavy flaking in a cast iron line means the wall has thinned, which is why a plumber may suggest replacing a section rather than patching it.

A ground warning to report separately

According to the Shelby County Reporter in April 2014, limestone bedrock underlies much of Shelby County, so sinkholes are common. That year a sinkhole opened on Cambrian Ridge Drive in Pelham after heavy flooding, with a void about four feet wide and 22 feet deep beneath a surface depression only two feet across.

If you see a bowl-shaped depression, a leaning tree or a dip in a paved road that holds water near a road or building, keep people away and call the police, who alert Public Works.

That is a ground warning, not a sewer diagnosis. If a depression sits above a line you suspect is damaged, tell the plumber about it when you call. Dial 811 before any digging, and plan to ask the Pelham Building Department at 205.620.6495 about permits and fees. The department also lists a sewer impact fee for projects that connect to the sewer, so ask whether anything applies to a repair.
