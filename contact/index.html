What to tell the dispatcher

  • Your street address, town and ZIP, plus a nearby cross road.
  • What you see: water, sewage, no hot water, a slow drain or a smell.
  • Where it is: kitchen, bathroom, basement, water heater closet or outside.
  • Whether you have shut off the main water valve and whether anything is still running.
  • A good callback number and how to get in, such as a gate or a pet.

While you wait

If water is flowing, close the main water valve at the house. If you cannot find it or reach it safely, call and the dispatcher will talk you through it. Once the main is off and it is safe, you can open a low faucet to drain the lines.

If standing water is near outlets, appliances or a panel, keep out and call. Switch off power to the area only if you can reach the breaker from dry footing. For a sewage backup, stop using every drain and toilet. If you smell gas, leave first and call your gas utility or 911.

Move rugs and valuables out of the wet area and take photos before you clean up. Ask your insurer what your policy covers and keep photos and the plumber's invoice. Photos and invoices also matter for a Pelham Water Works leak adjustment, where the policy at time of writing asks for repair documentation. Confirm current terms with Pelham Water Works.
