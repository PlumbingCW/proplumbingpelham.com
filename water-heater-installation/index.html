Water heater installation is the work of setting a new heater, whether it replaces an old one or goes into a new room, and connecting its water, fuel, vent and safety parts so it runs correctly. If you are planning ahead, a licensed plumber can walk through the options by phone, and same-day appointments when available can cover a first look at the space. Photos of the heater's location help on that call.

This page is for planned work. If an old tank is spilling water right now, the faster path is replacing a failed water heater, which covers shutting off water and power and what the plumber does that day.

Tank, hybrid or tankless: choosing what goes in

Each type trades up-front work against how it behaves at the tap. A standard tank is the simplest and is usually the easiest to fit in an existing closet. A hybrid pulls heat from the surrounding air, so it needs space and airflow. A tankless unit heats water as it flows, so it needs the right gas line or electrical supply and exhaust venting.

How the three types compare for a planned install
TypeWhat it needsTrade-off to weigh
Standard tank, gas or electricA drain pan, relief valve pipe, shutoffs and a vent or circuitSimple and familiar, but it holds hot water and can run out
Hybrid heat pump tankAir clearance, a condensate drain and a dedicated electric circuitNeeds room and makes some noise, but suits a garage or large closet
Tankless, gas or electricA suitably sized gas line or high-amp circuit, plus venting for gasEndless hot water at a set flow rate, with more install work and scale care

If you are leaning toward on-demand heating, read the tankless water heater page before you decide. Flow rate, simultaneous use and upkeep are where those units differ most.

Venting and gas line checks before anything is connected

A gas heater is only as safe as its exhaust path. The plumber checks that the vent is the right size and pitch, is sealed at the joints and ends at a safe point outside. Old vents from a previous heater are not always reusable. A new unit may draw differently, so a flue that worked for the old one can need a liner or replacement.

The gas line is checked for size and condition. A heater that needs more gas than the pipe can deliver will starve and shut off at the worst times. Tankless gas units need the most from the line, which is why a line upgrade is common with that type.

Gas and combustion work is regulated. Alabama's Plumbers and Gas Fitters Examining Board Rule 720-X-1-.02 names the 2021 International Residential Code for residential plumbing and gas installations, the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) for commercial plumbing, and the 2021 International Fuel Gas Code for commercial gas. That is how the rule is written, so ask the board how it applies to your job and check it for the current edition.

Small parts plumbers check on a new heater

  • Expansion tank. In a closed plumbing system, heated water has nowhere to expand. A small tank on the cold line takes up the pressure so the relief valve does not weep.
  • Drain pan and pipe. A pan under the heater catches slow leaks, and its drain line sends them to a safe spot.
  • Shutoff valves. A working cold shutoff above the heater lets any future job start without closing the whole house.
  • Relief valve and discharge pipe. The pipe runs to a safe point and stays uncapped.
  • Flexible connectors and dielectric fittings. New ones prevent the corrosion that forms where unlike metals meet.

These parts cost little compared with the heater, and a leaky one gets expensive quickly. Ask the plumber which of them are already in place and which are new.

Relocating the heater or hooking one up in new construction

Moving a heater from a cramped closet to a garage or utility room can make service easier and open up living space. It also means running new water lines, a new vent or circuit, and possibly a new condensate or pan drain. Each of those has its own routing questions.

In new construction the work comes in stages. The rough-in puts in supply lines, the gas stub and the vent path before walls close. The heater itself is set and tested later. If you are the homeowner or builder, ask the plumber to confirm the exact stub locations on the plans before drywall goes up.

Whole-house projects that include a heater among other fixtures fall under plumbing installation, where the plumber handles fixtures, supply and drain runs together.

What to have ready before the plumber arrives

  1. Clear a path

    Move storage away from the heater, the route to it and the area where the old unit will be carried out.

  2. Find the shutoffs

    Know where your main water valve and your gas shutoff are. Show them to the plumber at the start.

  3. Note the old label

    The rating plate is a sticker on the side of the tank. Photograph it so the plumber can read the fuel, capacity and model it was set up for.

  4. Plan for a short outage

    Hot water is off for part of the visit, so schedule laundry and showers around it.

  5. Decide on extras

    Choose in advance whether you want a new pan, valves or expansion tank, so the price you agreed holds.
